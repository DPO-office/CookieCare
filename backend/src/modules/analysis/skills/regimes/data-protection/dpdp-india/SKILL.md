# Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (India) overlay

## Coverage
Primary sources are the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (No. 22 of 2023) and the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E), dated 13 November 2025). This pack covers the Data Fiduciary / Data Processor contractual relationship — engagement basis, accountability, security, breach intimation, erasure flow-down, data quality, grievance redressal, children's data, cross-border transfer, and Significant Data Fiduciary duties. It does not cover Data Protection Board procedure, Appellate Tribunal process, or Consent Manager registration mechanics, which are regulator/registration matters rather than contract clauses.

## rule:dpdpa.df.valid_processor_contract
A Data Fiduciary may engage, appoint, use or otherwise involve a Data Processor to process personal data on its behalf for any activity related to offering goods or services to Data Principals only under a valid contract (section 8(2)).

## rule:dpdpa.df.fiduciary_accountability
The Data Fiduciary remains responsible for complying with the Act and rules in respect of any processing undertaken by it or on its behalf by a Data Processor, irrespective of any agreement to the contrary or the Data Principal's failure to carry out her duties (section 8(1)).

## rule:dpdpa.df.security_safeguards
The Data Fiduciary must protect personal data in its possession or under its control, including processing by its Data Processor, through reasonable security safeguards — data security measures, access controls, logging/monitoring, and continued-processing/backup measures (section 8(5); rule 6).

## rule:dpdpa.df.breach_intimation
On becoming aware of a personal data breach, the Data Fiduciary must intimate the Data Protection Board of India without delay (with updated details within 72 hours or a Board-approved extension) and intimate each affected Data Principal without delay, in a concise, clear and plain manner (section 8(6); rule 7).

## rule:dpdpa.df.erasure_flowdown
Where a Data Principal withdraws consent or the specified purpose is no longer served, the Data Fiduciary must, within a reasonable time, itself cease and cause its Data Processor to cease processing, and cause the Data Processor to erase any personal data made available to it, unless retention is necessary for compliance with law (sections 6(6) and 8(7)(b)).

## rule:dpdpa.df.data_quality
Where personal data is likely to be used to make a decision affecting the Data Principal, or is likely to be disclosed to another Data Fiduciary, the processing Data Fiduciary must ensure its completeness, accuracy and consistency (section 8(3)).

## rule:dpdpa.df.grievance_redressal
The Data Fiduciary must establish an effective mechanism to redress Data Principal grievances, publish the business contact information of a Data Protection Officer or other responsible person, and respond to grievances within the prescribed period, not exceeding ninety days under the Rules (sections 8(9), 8(10) and 13; rules 9 and 14).

## rule:dpdpa.df.children_consent
Before processing personal data of a child (or a person with disability who has a lawful guardian), the Data Fiduciary must obtain verifiable consent of the parent or lawful guardian, and must not undertake tracking or behavioural monitoring of children or targeted advertising directed at children, subject to notified exemptions (section 9(1)-(3); rules 10-12).

## rule:dpdpa.df.cross_border_transfer
Personal data may be transferred outside India, but the Data Fiduciary must comply with any Central Government notification restricting transfer to a specified country or territory, and with any requirements specified for making personal data available to a foreign State or an entity/agency under its control (section 16; rule 15).

## rule:dpdpa.sdf.significant_fiduciary_duties
A Significant Data Fiduciary must appoint a Data Protection Officer based in India who is responsible to its board or governing body and is the grievance-redressal point of contact, appoint an independent data auditor, and undertake periodic Data Protection Impact Assessment and audit (section 10; rule 13).

## risk:dpdpa_processor_contract_gap
The Data Processor's engagement is not grounded in a valid, executed contract.

## risk:dpdpa_fiduciary_accountability_gap
The contract purports to shift the Data Fiduciary's own statutory accountability to the Data Processor or condition it on the Data Principal's performance.

## risk:dpdpa_security_safeguards_gap
No concrete data security, access-control, or detection/continuity measures are required for personal data.

## risk:dpdpa_breach_notification_gap
No 72-hour Data Protection Board intimation path or Data Principal breach-notification duty.

## risk:dpdpa_erasure_flowdown_gap
The Data Processor is not obligated to cease processing and erase personal data on the Data Fiduciary's instruction.

## risk:dpdpa_data_quality_gap
Decisional or onward-disclosed personal data carries no completeness/accuracy/consistency standard.

## risk:dpdpa_grievance_redressal_gap
No effective grievance-redressal mechanism, published contact, or response timeline.

## risk:dpdpa_children_consent_gap
No verifiable parental/guardian consent mechanism, or no prohibition on tracking/behavioural monitoring/targeted advertising directed at children.

## risk:dpdpa_cross_border_transfer_gap
Cross-border transfer is unconditional and does not account for Central Government transfer restrictions.

## risk:dpdpa_significant_fiduciary_gap
No India-based Data Protection Officer, independent data auditor, or periodic DPIA/audit for a Significant Data Fiduciary.
