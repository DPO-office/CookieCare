## gdpr-art28/skill.md
 
### When this applies
A party processes personal data on behalf of another party (controller/processor relationship) within scope of GDPR.
 
### Requirements
- MUST use only processors providing sufficient guarantees to implement appropriate technical and organisational measures meeting GDPR. (Art. 28(1))
- MUST NOT allow the processor to engage another processor without the controller's prior specific or general written authorisation; if general authorisation is given, the processor must inform the controller of intended changes and give the controller the chance to object. (Art. 28(2))
- MUST govern the processing by a written contract (including electronic form) that sets out: the subject-matter and duration of processing, the nature and purpose of processing, the type of personal data and categories of data subjects, and the controller's obligations and rights. (Art. 28(3), (9))
- The contract MUST stipulate that the processor:
  - (a) processes personal data only on documented instructions from the controller, including for international transfers, unless required otherwise by law — and if so required, informs the controller of that legal requirement first, unless prohibited on public-interest grounds. (Art. 28(3)(a))
  - (b) ensures authorised persons are committed to confidentiality or under a statutory confidentiality obligation. (Art. 28(3)(b))
  - (c) takes all Article 32 security measures. (Art. 28(3)(c))
  - (d) respects the conditions for engaging a sub-processor set out in Art. 28(2) and (4). (Art. 28(3)(d))
  - (e) assists the controller, insofar as possible, in responding to data-subject rights requests. (Art. 28(3)(e))
  - (f) assists the controller with compliance under Arts. 32-36 (security, breach notification, DPIAs, prior consultation). (Art. 28(3)(f))
  - (g) at the controller's choice, deletes or returns all personal data after services end, and deletes existing copies unless law requires retention. (Art. 28(3)(g))
  - (h) makes available all information necessary to demonstrate compliance and allows for and contributes to audits, including inspections, by the controller or its mandated auditor. (Art. 28(3)(h))
- MUST require the processor to immediately inform the controller if it believes an instruction infringes GDPR or other data-protection law. (Art. 28(3), final subparagraph)
- MUST flow down the same data-protection obligations to any sub-processor via a written contract; the original processor remains fully liable to the controller for the sub-processor's performance. (Art. 28(4))
- SHOULD note that adherence to an approved code of conduct or certification mechanism may help demonstrate sufficient guarantees. (Art. 28(5))
- SHOULD note the contract may be based, in whole or part, on Commission or supervisory-authority standard contractual clauses. (Art. 28(6)-(8))
- Flag: if the processor determines the purposes and means of processing itself, it is considered a controller for that processing, not a processor. (Art. 28(10)) — a red-flag condition worth surfacing to a human if detected mid-draft.
