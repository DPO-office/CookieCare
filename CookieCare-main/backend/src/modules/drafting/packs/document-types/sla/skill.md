# Service Level Agreement

**When to use**: Use when a Service Provider and Customer wish to document measurable service availability commitments, support response obligations, and the remedies (service credits) that apply when targets are not met. Often attached as an exhibit to an MSA or SaaS Agreement.

**Standard section list**
1. Parties
2. Definitions
3. Service Levels
4. Service Credits
5. Support and Incident Response
6. Miscellaneous

**Requirements**
- MUST identify the Service Provider and Customer by full legal name and state the SLA effective date.
- MUST define Availability (or Uptime), Downtime, Scheduled Maintenance, and Measurement Period as capitalised defined terms.
- MUST exclude Scheduled Maintenance, Customer-caused outages, and force majeure events from Downtime calculations.
- MUST state the monthly (or annual) availability commitment as a percentage (e.g. 99.9% of Measurement Period minutes).
- MUST define the measurement methodology: how Downtime is tracked, reported, and validated.
- MUST include a credit request procedure specifying the claim window (typically within 30 days of the affected period) and required documentation.
- MUST define service credit tiers as a percentage of monthly fees, escalating with breach severity (e.g. 10% credit for 99.5%+ availability, 25% for below 99%).
- MUST cap total service credits in any calendar month at a stated maximum (e.g. 30% of monthly fees) to limit Provider exposure.
- MUST state that service credits are the Customer sole and exclusive financial remedy for availability failures and do not limit other termination rights.
- MUST classify incident severity levels (P1/Critical, P2/High, P3/Medium, P4/Low) with definitions for each.
- MUST specify initial response time commitments for each severity level (e.g. P1: within 1 hour, P2: within 4 hours).
- MUST specify target time-to-resolution or escalation timelines for each severity level.
- MUST define the support channel (ticketing system, email, telephone) and support hours (e.g. 24x7 for P1, business hours for P4).
- MUST include a right for Customer to terminate for cause if Provider fails to meet the SLA in any 3 consecutive calendar months.
- MUST state governing law and jurisdiction consistent with the principal agreement.
- SHOULD include provisions for scheduled maintenance notification periods (minimum advance notice, e.g. 5 business days for non-emergency maintenance).
- SHOULD include an obligation on Provider to provide monthly SLA performance reports.

**Exhibits normally attached**
- Exhibit A: Service Description and Measurement Details (when used standalone)
