## Executive summary

This review checked 14 requirements. 5 are present, including Confidentiality of authorised persons, Processor security measures, and Processor assistance with data-subject rights, and 2 more. 8 are only partial, including Prior subprocessor authorisation, Mandatory processing agreement particulars, and Documented instructions-only processing, and 5 more. 1 was not found, including Processor due diligence. The reviewed requirements are not fully satisfied.

The Data Processing Agreement satisfies core written form, subprocessor flow-down, security, and data-subject rights assistance rules under GDPR Article 28. However, several mandatory elements are incomplete, including the unprovided Schedule A specifications for processing details, missing controller objection rights for subprocessor updates, missing statutory legal-mandate provisos, and absent unlawful-instruction warnings. Remediation is required to attach Schedule A and incorporate the missing statutory pro-controller safeguards.

Present: 5; Partial: 8; Gap: 1

## Key risks and priorities

### **Processor due diligence** (Article 28(1))

**Status:** ⚠️ **Gap**

**Expected standard**

A controller may use only processors providing sufficient guarantees that appropriate technical and organisational measures will protect data-subject rights and meet GDPR requirements.

**What the document provides**

The provided contract excerpts do not contain explicit operative guarantees or warranties from the Vendor (Processor) ensuring appropriate technical and organisational measures for GDPR compliance and data-subject rights protection under the main controller-processor relationship. The provided passages contain sub-processor due diligence obligations ('Carry out adequate due diligence...'), but do not establish an explicit commitment or operative rule that the Controller will select/use only processors providing sufficient GDPR guarantees.

**Key gap**

**Operative selection, warranty, or assurance text from the Vendor giving sufficient guarantees that appropriate technical and organisational measures will be implemented to meet GDPR requirements and protect data-subject rights.** Operative text establishing the Controller's explicit commitment to select or use only processors providing sufficient guarantees under GDPR Article 28(1).

### **Prior subprocessor authorisation** (Article 28(2))

**Status:** ⚠️ **Partial**

**Expected standard**

The processor must obtain the controller's specific or general prior written authorisation before appointing a subprocessor; under general authorisation it must give advance notice of additions or replacements so the controller can object.

**What the document provides**

The contract establishes that the Vendor cannot engage or replace a sub-processor without obtaining specific or general written approval from HCL in advance. The contract requires 90 days prior written notice of the appointment of any new sub-processor or change in sub-processor with full details of processing. The contract provides for 90 days advance written notice of subprocessor changes, but does not explicitly state a right or mechanism for HCL to object.

**Key gap**

**No explicit language grants the controller a right or opportunity to object to subprocessor additions or replacements.**

### **Mandatory processing agreement particulars** (Article 28(3))

**Status:** ⚠️ **Partial**

**Expected standard**

Processing must be governed by a binding contract or legal act in writing that states subject matter, duration, nature, purpose, personal-data types, data-subject categories, and the controller's obligations and rights.

**What the document provides**

The contract refers to Schedule A for the subject-matter of processing. The contract defines the duration of processing as specified in the Contractual Terms or Schedule A, surviving termination for as long as processing continues. The contract points to Schedule A for the nature and purpose of processing. The contract states that personal data types are specified in Schedule A. The contract references Schedule A for data subject categories. No explicit controller obligations and rights were established in the provided evidence passages.

**Key gap**

**The actual text or schedule defining the substantive subject matter is missing from the provided evidence passages.** The substantive text enumerating the processing operations (nature) and specific objectives (purpose) is missing from the provided text. The actual list or categories of personal data being processed are not included in the supplied evidence. The list of specific categories of data subjects is not present in the supplied passages. Specific controller duties (e.g. giving lawful instructions) and rights (e.g. audit entitlements) are not located in the supplied text.

### **Documented instructions-only processing** (Article 28(3)(a))

**Status:** ⚠️ **Partial**

**Expected standard**

The processor must process personal data, including transfers, only on documented controller instructions unless applicable law requires processing; where permitted, it must notify the controller of that legal requirement before processing.

**What the document provides**

The contract explicitly mandates that Vendor shall only process Personal Data on behalf of HCL in accordance with the Agreement and any other written instructions received from HCL, and prohibits processing for Vendor's own purposes. The contract requires prior written authorization for processing personal data outside the country where it is received, but does not explicitly contain the legal mandate proviso.

**Key gap**

**The contract lacks the explicit statutory proviso that transfers required by Union or Member State law are permitted only if the processor notifies the controller of that legal requirement before processing (unless prohibited by law).** Requires prior written authorization for cross-border processing, but omits the exception and prior notification mechanism for law-mandated transfers.

### **Subprocessor conditions and flow-down** (Article 28(3)(d))

**Status:** ⚠️ **Partial**

**Expected standard**

The processor must comply with Article 28(2) and (4): obtain required authorisation, impose the same data-protection obligations on each subprocessor by contract, and remain fully liable to the controller for subprocessor performance.

**What the document provides**

The contract explicitly prohibits Vendor from engaging or replacing a subprocessor without obtaining prior general or specific written approval from HCL. The agreement establishes a 90-day prior written notice requirement for new subprocessors or changes. Vendor must ensure each subprocessor is governed by a written contract containing data protection terms offering at least the same level of protection as the primary agreement.

**Key gap**

**None.** The contract does not establish an explicit right or mechanism for the controller to object to new subprocessors or changes. None. Notice is required 90 days in advance, but no explicit right to object is stipulated in the provided clause.

### **Processor assistance with security, breach, DPIA, and consultation duties** (Article 28(3)(f))

**Status:** ⚠️ **Partial**

**Expected standard**

Taking account of processing nature and available information, the processor must assist the controller with applicable Articles 32-36 obligations, including security, breach response, DPIAs, and any legally required prior consultation.

**What the document provides**

### **Return or deletion after services** (Article 28(3)(g))

**Status:** ⚠️ **Partial**

**Expected standard**

At the controller's choice, the processor must delete or return all personal data after services end, delete existing copies, and retain data only where applicable law requires storage.

**What the document provides**

The contract grants HCL the right to request return and/or destruction of all originals and copies of HCL Data upon expiration or termination or at any time. Vendor is obligated to destroy or dispose of data upon HCL's request or upon expiration/termination. Vendor is obligated to return a complete copy of all HCL Personal Data upon request or upon expiration/termination. The text references compliance with applicable laws and regulations generally, but does not explicitly state that existing copies must be deleted unless Union or Member State law requires continued storage.

**Key gap**

**An explicit proviso stating that existing copies will be deleted unless Union or Member State law requires storage of the personal data.**

### **Compliance evidence, audits, and unlawful-instruction warning** (Article 28(3)(h))

**Status:** ⚠️ **Partial**

**Expected standard**

The processor must provide all information needed to demonstrate Article 28 compliance, allow and contribute to controller or mandated-auditor audits and inspections, and immediately tell the controller if an instruction infringes GDPR or other applicable data-protection law.

**What the document provides**

The contract obligates the Vendor to make available all information necessary to demonstrate compliance and to allow for and contribute to audits, including inspections, by the controller or its mandated auditor. None located in the reviewed evidence passages.

**Key gap**

**The contract text does not contain an explicit obligation for the processor to immediately inform the controller if, in its opinion, an instruction infringes GDPR or other data protection laws.**

### **Processor acting as controller** (Article 28(10))

**Status:** ⚠️ **Partial**

**Expected standard**

A processor that determines processing purposes and means contrary to GDPR is treated as a controller for that processing and assumes controller obligations.

**What the document provides**

The contract limits Vendor's processing strictly to acting on behalf of HCL in accordance with the Agreement and written instructions, prohibiting processing for Vendor's own purposes. The contract explicitly prohibits the Vendor from processing data for its own purposes.

**Key gap**

**The contract does not contain explicit statutory re-characterisation language or state that determining purposes and means contrary to instructions causes the processor to assume full controller duties/responsibilities for that processing.**

## Conclusion

The reviewed requirements are not fully satisfied. The Data Processing Agreement satisfies core written form, subprocessor flow-down, security, and data-subject rights assistance rules under GDPR Article 28. However, several mandatory elements are incomplete, including the unprovided Schedule A specifications for processing details, missing controller objection rights for subprocessor updates, missing statutory legal-mandate provisos, and absent unlawful-instruction warnings. Remediation is required to attach Schedule A and incorporate the missing statutory pro-controller safeguards. This conclusion is limited to DPA - 5.pdf.
